# Unslop

Edit the supplied draft, not the facts or scope. Preserve intended voice, useful uncertainty, required headings and labels, exact commands and identifiers, links, quotations, code, and evidence. Repository and deliverable conventions override the defaults below. Leave good sentences alone.

## Edit the draft

- **Cut filler.** Remove agreement openers, preambles, generic conclusions, chatbot signoffs, and trailing clauses that add no fact. Replace “in order to” with “to”.
- **Replace vague claims with facts already in the draft.** Name the actor, mechanism, or result. Attribute evidence to its source. If the source or number is missing, preserve the uncertainty rather than inventing precision or making an unsupported claim sound firm.
- **Use plain language.** Prefer “use” over “utilize” and “is” over “serves as”. Use one name per thing. Keep domain terms when they are the precise words; cut buzzwords and metaphors that hide what happened.
- **Break formulaic patterns.** State the point instead of “not just X, but Y”. Do not group unrelated ideas into threes, bold every label, or decorate headings with emojis. Split sentences that require rereading. Use periods or commas in place of prose dashes.

Return the edited draft in the caller's format. For a workflow that called this skill, give back the draft without an editing commentary. The pass is done when the prose is clearer and all claims, qualifications, evidence, and required structure still mean the same thing.
